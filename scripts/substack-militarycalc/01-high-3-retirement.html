Your military pension is built on 36 months. Most people guess the wrong 36.

How High-3 really works, and a two-minute check you can run on your own number tonight.

Picture it. You are eighteen months from your 20-year mark. Someone at the unit asks what your retirement check will be, and you do the math in your head: last paycheck, times half. It sounds about right. You say it out loud, and you start planning a mortgage around it.

Why "last paycheck times half" feels right and isn't

Your pension is not based on your final check. It is based on the average of your highest 36 months of basic pay. That is what "High-3" means.

Three things push that average below the number in your head:

  • Basic pay only. Housing (BAH), food (BAS), special pays and bonuses are not in it. If you look at the total deposit on your LES, you are starting from the wrong line.
  • It is an average. A promotion or pay raise in your last year only counts for those months. The older, lower months pull the average down.
  • The multiplier is not always 50 percent. It depends on your years of service and which retirement system you are under.

None of this is hidden. It is just easy to skip when the mental math feels close enough.

The formula, with one worked example

Monthly pension = High-3 average × years of service × multiplier
Legacy High-3 multiplier: 2.5% per year. Blended Retirement System (BRS): 2.0% per year.

Here is an example with round numbers so the logic is easy to follow. These are not real pay tables, just a model.

StepExample
Basic pay, oldest 12 of your top 36 months$5,000 / month
Basic pay, middle 12 months$5,200 / month
Basic pay, last 12 months (after a raise)$5,600 / month
High-3 average$5,267 / month
Legacy, 20 years: 20 × 2.5% = 50%about $2,633 / month
BRS, 20 years: 20 × 2.0% = 40%about $2,107 / month

Now the "last paycheck times half" version: $5,600 × 50% = $2,800. That is $167 a month too high for a legacy retiree in this example, and almost $700 too high if you are under BRS. Over a 30-year mortgage, that gap is not a rounding error.

Legacy High-3 vs. Blended Retirement: same years, different check

If you joined after January 1, 2018, or opted in during the window, you are under BRS. The trade is a lower multiplier (2.0% instead of 2.5%) in exchange for government matching in your TSP and a continuation pay option around year 12.

Legacy High-3Blended Retirement (BRS)
Multiplier per year2.5%2.0%
At 20 years50% of High-340% of High-3
TSP government matchNoYes, up to 5%
Continuation payNoYes, around year 12

That is why two people with the same rank and the same 20 years can have very different monthly checks. One is not wrong. They are on different systems. Before you compare numbers with anyone, ask which system they are on.

Check yourself: five questions

Grab your latest LES and answer these. If you cannot answer one, that is the one to fix first.

☐Check
☐Am I under Legacy High-3 or BRS?
☐What was my basic pay (not total pay) in each of my highest 36 months, or roughly in each of the last three years?
☐How many years and months of service will I have on my planned retirement date?
☐Is my multiplier 2.5% or 2.0% per year?
☐Does the number I have been telling people match High-3 × years × multiplier?

If question 5 is "no", you are in good company. Most people find a gap the first time they do this.

If you already planned around the wrong number

Take a breath. This is fixable, and the earlier you catch it, the cheaper it is.

  1. Today: recompute with the real High-3 and your actual multiplier. Write the new monthly number down next to the old one.
  2. This week: if a loan, lease or budget was sized on the old number, check how much room you have. A difference of a few hundred dollars a month often means adjusting the down payment or term, not cancelling the plan.
  3. Before you separate: request your official retirement estimate through your branch's retirement office or DFAS, and compare it with your own math. If they differ, ask why before your date, not after.
  4. Remember the other deductions: federal tax withholding and the Survivor Benefit Plan premium (if you elect it) also come out of the gross pension. Plan on the net.

Questions people actually type

Is my military pension based on my last paycheck?
No. It is based on the average of your highest 36 months of basic pay, not your final check and not your total pay.

Does BAH count toward High-3?
No. Only basic pay counts. Housing, food allowance and special pays are left out.

Why is my BRS pension lower than my friend's?
BRS uses 2.0% per year instead of 2.5%. The difference is meant to be made up by TSP matching, so look at both together.

What if I retire with 22 years instead of 20?
Each extra year adds another 2.5% (legacy) or 2.0% (BRS) of your High-3, and your High-3 itself may be higher by then.
