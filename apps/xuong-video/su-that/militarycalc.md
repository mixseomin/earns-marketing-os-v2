## Retirement (active duty, non-disability)
- Retired pay base for High-3 = the average of the member's highest 36 months of BASIC PAY. BAH, BAS, special pays and bonuses are not included.
- Legacy High-3 system: 2.5% per year of service. 20 years = 50%. Each year beyond 20 adds 2.5%. The multiplier can reach 100% at 40 years.
- Blended Retirement System (BRS): 2.0% per year of service. 20 years = 40%. BRS also adds government TSP contributions: 1% of basic pay automatically plus matching of up to 4% (5% total) when the member contributes 5%. BRS also has a continuation pay option around year 12.
- BRS applies to members who joined on or after January 1, 2018, and to those who opted in during the opt-in window; others are under Legacy High-3.
- A full non-disability retirement requires 20 years of service.
- Monthly pension (gross) = High-3 average x years of service x multiplier. Federal tax withholding and the Survivor Benefit Plan premium (if elected) come out of the gross.
- The official estimate comes from the branch retirement office / DFAS; tell readers to compare their own math with it before their retirement date.

## Guard / Reserve drill pay
- One drill period (a training block of at least four hours) pays 1/30 of the monthly active-duty basic pay for the member's grade and years of service.
- A normal drill weekend is four drill periods (two on Saturday, two on Sunday), so one weekend pays about four days of basic pay before tax.
- Annual training is paid as active-duty days.
- Drill pay is basic pay; federal tax is withheld.

## BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing)
- BAH depends on the ZIP code of the permanent duty station, the pay grade, and dependency status (with or without dependents). The number of dependents does not change the rate.
- DoD sets new rates every year from local rent and utility surveys; new rates start January 1. Members cover a small share of housing cost themselves.
- Rate protection: if the area rate drops, the member keeps the higher rate while staying at the same duty station. A PCS move resets it to the new station's rate.
- BAH is a tax-free allowance: not part of federal taxable income, not in box 1 of the W-2. Base pay is taxed; BAH and BAS are not.
- Homeowners can still deduct mortgage interest and property taxes even if they pay them with BAH.
- Overseas assignments use OHA, not BAH.
