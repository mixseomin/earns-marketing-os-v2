## How these scams work
- Messages often use the guest's real name, real dates and part of the real booking number, so bad spelling is no longer a reliable sign.
- Some arrive inside a booking site's own chat after a hotel's partner account has been taken over; others come by WhatsApp, SMS or email.
- They lean on three levers: a clock running out, fear of losing a stay already paid for, and a name the guest recognises.

## Five red flags
1. A deadline you never agreed to ("cancelled within 24 hours unless you act"). A confirmed booking is not cancelled by a chat message with a countdown. Check: open the app or site you booked with; if it says confirmed, it is confirmed.
2. A payment link, a QR code, or a "new" bank account. Hotels do not move their bank account by email, and you never need a link from a chat to pay. Pay only on the booking site itself or at the front desk.
3. A request for card numbers, one-time codes (OTP) or ID photos. A one-time code is the bank's confirmation that you approve a payment; whoever has it can spend your money. Never send these in a chat.
4. Being moved off the booking app ("our app chat is unavailable, continue on WhatsApp"). Leaving the platform leaves its protection behind. Keep every money conversation inside the booking app.
5. A sender that doesn't match: a random mobile number, a web address with extra words like "secure-pay" or "verify", or no hotel name. Find the hotel's official website yourself and call the number listed there, never the number in the message.

## A real message looks like
- A confirmation with no link to pay, no deadline, nothing sensitive asked for, that sends you back to your own booking app.

## If you already clicked or paid (in this order)
- Stop replying; don't argue or send anything else. Take screenshots of the messages first.
- Call your bank on the number printed on the back of your card; ask them to block the card and dispute the payment. Banks move faster the sooner they hear.
- If you typed a password on the linked page, change that password now, and anywhere else you use it.
- Tell the booking site through its app, so it can warn the hotel and flag the account that messaged you.
- Check your booking in the app: in most cases it is still there and still confirmed.
- Watch your statement for the next few weeks for small test charges.

## Common questions
- Paying a hotel over WhatsApp: no. Pay on the booking site or at the property.
- A confirmed booking is cancelled through the booking site, and you see it there; a message threatening cancellation unless you pay through a link is the scam itself.
- Scammers know names and dates because a hotel's account on a booking site sometimes gets taken over and they read real reservations. Judge a message by what it asks you to do.
- Deposit by bank transfer: only when the booking site itself shows that payment method on the booking page, never because a message asked.
