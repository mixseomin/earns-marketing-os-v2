What one drill weekend actually pays (and why the deposit looks smaller than you expected)

Drill periods, the 1/30 rule, and a two-minute check for Guard and Reserve members.

Imagine your first drill weekend after a promotion. You worked Saturday and Sunday, you know your new rank, and you expect the deposit to reflect it. It shows up, and it looks like a weekend at a part-time job, not a soldier's paycheck.

Nothing is broken. Drill pay is counted in a way most people never get explained, and once you see the rule, the number makes sense.

In two minutes you will know how a drill weekend is counted, what one weekend is worth at your pay grade, and where to check your own figure.

The rule in one line: one drill period pays 1/30 of the monthly active-duty basic pay for your grade and years of service. A normal weekend is four drill periods.

Why the weekend deposit surprises people

Most of us think in "days". The pay system thinks in drill periods. A drill period is a block of training of at least four hours. Saturday is usually two periods, Sunday two more. That is the four-period weekend you will see written as MUTA-4.

Each period is paid like one day of active-duty basic pay. So a full weekend pays roughly four days of basic pay, not a quarter of a month.

Two more things shrink the deposit compared with what people picture:

  • Basic pay only. On a normal drill weekend you are not drawing a full month of housing or food allowance the way active duty does.
  • Taxes still come out. Federal withholding applies to drill pay like any other pay.

Check yourself: four questions

☐What is my current pay grade and years of service for pay?
☐What is the monthly basic pay for that grade and those years?
☐How many drill periods did I actually get credit for this month?
☐Does my deposit roughly match periods × (monthly basic pay ÷ 30), minus tax?

If the last answer is "no" by more than a little, write down the month and the number of periods. That is what your unit admin will ask for.

If your drill pay looks wrong

Keep it calm and specific. Most problems are a missing period or a grade change that has not hit the system yet.

  1. Today: compare the periods you attended with the periods on your pay record for that month.
  2. This week: if you were promoted, check that the new grade's effective date is showing. Back pay usually follows once it is.
  3. Then: bring the month, the periods and the amount to your unit admin or pay office. A short, exact note gets fixed faster than "my pay seems low".

Questions people actually type

How much is one drill weekend?
About four days of active-duty basic pay for your grade and years of service, before tax.

Is one drill period one day?
No. A period is a training block of at least four hours. A normal day of drill is usually two periods.

Why was my annual training month bigger?
Annual training is paid as active-duty days, so it is counted differently from drill periods.

One thing to do before you close this tab

Look up the monthly basic pay for your grade, divide by 30, and multiply by the periods you drilled. If it matches your deposit, you are done. If not, you now know exactly what to ask.
